# The Scroll

Karl writes for someone who may not remember tomorrow. Every term,
every cancellation, every algebraic step must be explicit — because
the scroll is the only memory we have.

The actual Karl Schwarzschild derived the first exact solution to
Einstein's field equations by hand, in a trench, while dying of
pemphigus in 1916. If he could write every step under those
conditions, we can do it in LaTeX.

**Principles for the schwarzchild document:**

- **No skipped steps.** Show every term in the Christoffel sum.
  Show every contraction in the Ricci tensor. Show what cancels
  and why. The reader should never wonder "where did that come
  from?"
- **Self-contained.** The derivation should require no external
  references. A motivated advanced undergraduate with vector
  calculus and some tensor familiarity should be able to follow
  from start to finish.
- **The tedious parts are the teaching parts.** Where the
  condensed version glides over algebra, this document drills
  in. That's where students get stuck, and that's where the
  exposition earns its keep.
- **Treat the exercises as the scroll's companions.** Every
  concept introduced in the derivation should have a companion
  exercise that tests understanding, builds fluency, or extends
  the result.

This file is the project's conscience. When tempted to write
"the remaining components vanish" or collapse a multi-line
expansion into a single equation, return here.
